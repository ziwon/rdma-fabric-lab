One switch never has to route between switches. B-02 adds a second switch and joins the two with two parallel links, so the SM has to choose an inter-switch port for every remote LID. The checks are those of B-01, and `routes-shortest` now has real choices to judge.

## Goal

Show that OpenSM discovers two switches, four HCAs and six links, that every switch forwards every remote LID across an inter-switch link one hop closer to it, and that every LID answers.

## Concepts it uses

- [The subnet manager](/concepts/infiniband-management/) and [linear forwarding tables](/concepts/infiniband-management/).
- [Routing engines](/concepts/infiniband-management/), minhop here, and why updn does not fit this fabric.

OpenSM runs with `-R minhop`. updn needs root switches, and on this single-tier fabric it finds none. A development run with `opensm -R updn` showed the fallback, and the `subnet-up` check failed that run, because the log named an engine other than the configured one (runs `20261115T141931Z` and `20261115T141940Z`, [ADR 0005](/docs/adr/0005-track-b-fabric-simulation/) section 2).

## Questions to think about

- With two parallel links, how should a good engine spread the remote LIDs between them, and how could you see the split in `ibroute`?
- OpenSM 3.3.23 rejects the `no_fallback` engine option. Why does that make the `subnet-up` check's engine test necessary?
- Every route here crosses at most one switch-to-switch link. Why does that make a credit loop impossible on this fabric even with minhop? The record calls this reasoning, not a measurement.

## What the result means and does not mean

The run shows OpenSM routing across two switches correctly as ibsim presents them.
